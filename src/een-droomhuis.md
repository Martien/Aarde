## {igokochi no yoi ouchi}
The Japanese language doesn't have a single, unique traditional term exclusively meaning "a good home" in the way English might use something idiomatic like "dream home" or "happy home."

However, several expressions and words convey the idea of a pleasant, comfortable, cozy, or ideal home:

- The most common way to describe a **good/comfortable/cozy home** is **居心地の良い家** ({igokochi no yoi ie}) or **居心地の良いおうち** ({igokochi no yoi ouchi}).
    - **居心地が良い** ({igokochi ga yoi}) means "comfortable to stay in," "pleasant atmosphere," or "feels good to be there" — it refers to a home where you feel relaxed, at ease, and welcomed.
    - This is widely used in everyday Japanese to praise a nice living space (e.g., "居心地の良い家ですね" = "What a comfortable/good home!").
- For a more casual or affectionate feel (especially one's own home), people often say **いい家** (ii ie) or **いいおうち**(ii ouchi), literally "good house/home."
    - **おうち** (ouchi) is a cute/informal version of **家** (ie/uchi), often carrying warmer, homier connotations.
- The English phrase "Home sweet home" is sometimes rendered as **スイートホーム** (suīto hōmu, a loanword), **楽しき我が家** (tanoshiki wagaya = "joyful/pleasant my home"), or casually **おうち最高！** (ouchi saikō! = "Home is the best!").
- In terms of nuance, **うち** (uchi) often implies a warmer, more personal/emotional "home" (with a sense of belonging and comfort) compared to the more neutral/physical **家** (ie). So **いいうち** can subtly suggest a "good/nice home" in an intimate way.

If you're referring to a specific cultural concept, Japanese homes often emphasize harmony, cleanliness, and natural flow (e.g., through principles like ma for space, or traditional elements like tatami for coziness), but no one fixed term stands out as "the" word for a good home beyond the practical phrases above.
